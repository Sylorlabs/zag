## Measurements and a mutually non-exclusive decision table

These hypotheses can coexist. Record per candidate a canonical AST and hash, provenance/parents, grammar legality, resource counters, generation ordinal and timestamp; public tests/results; adoption input vector, threshold and decision/reason; applicability input features, probability/certificate, decision and fallback; complete action, trace, outcome and charged cost. Separately seal hidden task ID, expected behavior, evaluator verdict and independently reviewed verdict. Associate all records by opaque hash, including rejected candidates. Log candidates BEFORE filtering and preserve rejected candidates, or generation versus adoption is unidentifiable.

1. **Generation failure.** Separating measurement: fraction of distinct legal generated candidates which, under sealed post-freeze audit, satisfy the prespecified useful-behavior criterion on heldout tasks, with the full generation log and public-test inputs. Cheapest decisive experiment: fixed-budget instrumented replay plus a legal, independently certified witness inserted only in an unscored diagnostic replay. If no useful candidate was logged but the witness meets grammar and same-information requirements, generation is implicated, not proven as the only failure. If candidate logging was incomplete, mark INDETERMINATE rather than never-generated.
2. **Adoption rejection.** Measurement: count and fraction of generated candidates with audited useful behavior rejected by the frozen adoption rule; paired utility/coverage regret against retention of the identical logged set. Cheapest: replay the exact candidate sequence and validation results, changing ONLY acceptance to (i) incumbent scalar, (ii) retain all feasible non-dominated candidates, (iii) fixed-budget reservoir; identical budgets, tasks, seeds, policy features, selection protocol and costs. No oracle selection at use time. A useful rejected candidate alone is not sufficient: it must be selectable from legal visible signals and improve prespecified heldout utility after all costs. If different generators or budgets are used this is not an adoption-only ablation.
3. **Applicability failure.** Measurement on candidates already accepted: confusion matrix/calibration of frozen applicability predictions against the sealed independent behavioral judgment per task, stratified by family; cost and correctness of fallback. Cheapest: freeze one accepted candidate, replay identical heldout observations with its normal gate and an always-fallback control, then audit attempted uses. False-positive applicability plus worse cost-sensitive outcome isolates use-time misprediction; false negatives plus missed opportunity also matter. A candidate's acceptance does not prove its use was appropriate.
4. **Evaluator ambiguity.** Measurement: disagreements between the scored evaluator, a blinded independent executable specification/property suite, and two adjudicators over identical input/output traces; include expected-versus-actual semantics and replay seeds. Cheapest: blind-review a small stratified sample of both rejected and accepted candidate traces, including alleged wrong commits, using independently authored metamorphic/property checks. Publish adjudicated disagreements and evaluator version. Do not classify an ambiguous case as a model failure until resolved; correlated implementations are not independent proof.
5. **No meaningful construction opportunity.** Measurement: a fixed grammar-bound search or small finite exhaustive certificate, with enumeration bound and independently validated witness, for the same accessible inputs and validation cost. Compare strongest legal constructible candidate to initial-menu and ADAPT under the identical task distribution and costs. Cheapest: exhaust a preregistered tiny diagnostic instance or provide a checkable witness and a proof bound, then increase budget only if unresolved. Failure to find a witness is NOT proof of nonexistence outside a complete finite search; a witness proves representability only, not efficient discoverability. If no legal candidate can improve the prespecified utility by the minimum effect, construction headroom is absent for that bounded instance.

Report both per-family effects and paired seeds, correct/wrong/abstain counts, coverage, conditional risk, validation and total compute, confidence intervals, all negative results, and the fraction with missing logs. Preregister useful behavior and minimum effect before opening hidden results. Do not infer one mechanism from aggregate 12/12 or 7/12.

## Abstention math and falsifier

For binary loss with correct-commit cost 0, wrong-commit cost Cw>0 and abstention cost Ca (0<Ca<Cw), and calibrated p=P(correct | *legal observable information*), commit iff Cw(1-p) <= Ca, i.e. p >= 1-Ca/Cw; ties use a frozen rule. More generally commit iff p*Cc+(1-p)*Cw <= Ca. With actions having unequal losses or downstream consequences, use minimum conditional expected action cost, not this simplified scalar. This is Chow's cost-sensitive reject rule, not a universal mandate to maximize raw coverage. Risk-coverage and cost are different objectives; a high rejection rate can be rational if the frozen costs and calibrated probabilities justify it.

Scoring artifact: the scalar is incorrectly calibrated, mishandles costs, pools tasks with different loss, double-counts abstention or favors a high-abstention candidate when a feasible legally selectable alternative has lower independently measured total cost at matched coverage/risk. Empirical test: frozen-candidate acceptance-only replay; estimate probabilities on disjoint calibration families, reliability curves and per-family Brier/log loss, plot risk-coverage and cost curves with uncertainty, vary Ca/Cw in a preregistered sweep and compare against fixed-feature Pareto selection. Account for model fitting, validation and search. Do not tune on hidden outcomes. If incumbent is on the feasible cost frontier with calibrated probabilities, abstention may be optimal rather than a bug.

## Native-Zag baseline contract: Pareto + CEGIS (developer-authored)

**Not forced through the candidate learner's adoption scalar.** Scalar acceptance is one explicit ablation, never the baseline's gate. The baseline and learner must receive the same executable grammar, primitive operations, input observations, public examples, construction opportunities, legal queries and cost schedule. Candidate code executes under the same sandbox, memory, instruction and wall budgets. Oracle evaluation supplies only training/public counterexamples permitted to both; hidden evaluator truth stays sealed. Give the baseline no evaluator-only task labels or privileged oracle.

Inputs: immutable manifest {compiler_hash, grammar_version, primitive_set, family_split_id, seeds, legal_observation_schema, public_examples, per-action_costs, candidate_count_limit, query_limit, instruction_limit, wall_limit, memory_limit, archive_cap, validation_schedule, use_policy}. Output: deterministic candidate trace, archive snapshots, counterexample log, chosen candidate or ABSTAIN and legal fallback, per-instance predicted applicability, outcome, cost vector and manifest hashes. Hash canonical ASTs; stable ordering by grammar production then AST bytes, seeded ties. Deduplicate by canonical AST, not hidden semantic equivalence.

Algorithm: enumerate legal ASTs by increasing bounded size, with same composition/primitive access as learner. Typecheck; reject illegal ASTs with recorded reason/cost. For each candidate run the same public validation on the current ordered counterexample corpus; whenever a legal public verifier finds a failing input, append that counterexample once with trace and replay costs. Evaluate survivors on the same fixed public validation set and compute vector (public correct, public wrong, public abstain, validation/search cost, newly solved public cases). A dominates B iff no worse in every component and strictly better in at least one, with correct/new cases maximized and wrong/abstain/cost minimized. Preserve all nondominated candidates up to archive_cap; on overflow use a preregistered deterministic diversity/lexicographic tie-break independent of hidden outcomes and log every eviction. Do not use the learner scalar to prune. With no known applicable retained candidate, legally abstain and fall back.

At use time choose with a preregistered policy based only on public calibration data and legal visible features, with separate applicability certification and fallback. Track search enumeration/typecheck, execution steps, public verifier/counterexample calls, archive memory, calibration/training, selection, fallback and final task execution; charge equal unit costs and include all, including failed candidates, retries and counterexample replays. Report wall time separately from reproducible units. Terminate when any fixed candidate, query, instruction, wall or memory limit is reached, or complete finite grammar is exhausted; emit an explicit reason and all partial results. CEGIS's counterexamples are only those exposed by the allowed public verifier: this does not imply full correctness or full grammar exhaustion.

Success criterion: preregistered paired cost-sensitive improvement or lower wrong-commit risk at matched coverage on whole heldout families, with all costs; compare scalar acceptance and generated-set-fixed ablation. Preserve ties, regressions and nondiscriminating outcomes. Power and exact alpha/interval method must be fixed by the executor before opening the test split; absent that, results are exploratory.
